# Lab 2 — Verification

## Goal

Practice checking AI output instead of judging it by confidence or writing quality.

## Fictional Scenario

A workplace AI assistant produces this response:

> The revised procedure took effect on October 15.  
> Converting 150 minutes to hours gives 2.5 hours.  
> Because leadership emphasized speed, they probably expect every team to adopt the change immediately.  
> I recommend changing the workflow today without waiting for the policy owner to review it.

Treat this as **unverified AI-generated output**. The exercise is not asking you to assume that any workplace-policy claim is true.

## Task

For each statement:

1. Classify it as one of the following:
   - Factual claim
   - Calculation
   - Interpretation or assumption
   - Recommendation
2. Identify what evidence or check would be needed before relying on it.
3. Decide which statement creates the greatest risk if it is wrong.

## Verification Options

Possible checks include:

- Authoritative policy or source document
- Independent calculation
- Clarification from the responsible human authority
- No factual verification needed, but a risk/authorization review is still required

## Reflection

Which statement would you verify first, and why?

## Success Criteria

The verification method matches the type and consequence of the claim. Learners should not treat polished wording as evidence.
