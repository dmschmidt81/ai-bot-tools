# 15-Minute Sample Lesson

## Why a Good Prompt Is Not Enough: Verifying AI Output

**Audience:** Mixed technical/nontechnical adults  
**Length:** 15 minutes  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate practical AI literacy and facilitation

## 0:00–2:00 — Hook

Ask:

> If an AI response is detailed, confident, and includes citations, what evidence do you actually have that it is correct?

Teaching point:

**Fluency is not verification.**

## 2:00–5:00 — Mental Model

Explain briefly:

- AI can generate useful text from context and learned patterns.
- It can also generate unsupported details.
- Better prompting reduces ambiguity but does not guarantee truth.

Write:

**Good prompt ≠ verified answer**

## 5:00–9:00 — Demonstration

Display this **fictional, deliberately unverified** AI output:

> The new workplace procedure began on October 15.  
> A 120-minute training session lasts 2 hours.  
> Because the memo emphasizes urgency, leadership probably wants every department to switch immediately.  
> I recommend changing the process today.

Ask learners to label each statement:

- factual claim,
- calculation,
- interpretation/assumption,
- or recommendation.

Then ask:

> Would you verify all four in the same way?

Introduce:

- factual claim → authoritative source
- calculation → recompute
- policy/procedure → governing document or responsible authority
- recommendation → evaluate evidence, risk, and authorization

**Instructor key:** the first statement is an unverified factual claim; the second is a calculation that can be independently checked; the third is an interpretation/assumption; the fourth is a recommendation that should not become an external action without appropriate review.

## 9:00–12:00 — Human Approval

Show:

```text
AI draft
   ↓
Verification
   ↓
Human approval
   ↓
Action
```

Ask:

> Where would the approval gate go if this were an email to 500 employees? What if it were only brainstorming?

Discuss consequence and proportional control.

## 12:00–14:00 — Learner Check

Give the scenario:

> AI summarizes a new workplace policy and recommends that employees change a procedure immediately.

Ask one question:

> What would you verify first, and why?

Listen for source-of-truth reasoning.

## 14:00–15:00 — Close

Leave learners with:

**Prompt → Evaluate → Verify → Improve → Govern → Apply**

Final statement:

> The goal is not to distrust AI. The goal is to know what evidence you need before trusting a particular output.
