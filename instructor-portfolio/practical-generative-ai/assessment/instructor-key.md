# Instructor Assessment Key

This guide supports consistent review of the workshop assessments. It is not intended as a rigid high-stakes grading key.

## Pre-Assessment

Use responses diagnostically.

Look for evidence that the learner already understands:

- fluent AI output can still require verification,
- citations should be opened and checked rather than trusted by appearance,
- drafting permission is different from permission to send or publish,
- sensitive information may require an approved system or should not be entered at all.

Do not assign a proficiency label from the pre-assessment alone.

## Post-Assessment — Expected Indicators

### Part 1

**Why can fluent AI output still be wrong?**

Strong responses recognize that language quality or confidence does not establish factual accuracy; generated content can contain unsupported assumptions or errors.

**Two ways to reduce unsupported assumptions**

Examples include:

- provide relevant context,
- identify the source of truth,
- specify constraints,
- define the desired output,
- require the model to flag missing information instead of guessing.

**Why recheck a correction?**

A correction can introduce a new error, fail to address the original problem, or create an inconsistency elsewhere.

### Part 2

For a policy-summary scenario, a proficient response should identify:

- the governing policy as the source of truth,
- a plausible failure mode such as omission, hallucination, outdated information, or overbroad interpretation,
- comparison with the actual policy as a verification method,
- human approval before consequential procedural change,
- and a responsible human decision-maker rather than the AI as final authority.

### Part 3

A proficient workflow should include a meaningful verification step and should not allow an AI draft to become an external action automatically when consequences require human approval.

## Scoring

Use the [Performance Rubric](./rubric.md).

Evidence should come from what the learner actually explains or produces, not from inferred intent.
